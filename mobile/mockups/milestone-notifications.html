What we decided

Flourish is a memory app. It is not CDC’s Milestone Tracker, and it is not a screening tool.

Sources

  • CDC Learn the Signs. Act Early. (Feb 2022 revision, with AAP). Ages when ≥75% of children can do the item. Surveillance, not screening. CDC is explicit that checklists are not a substitute for AAP-recommended screening at 9, 18 and 30 months (and autism screening at 18/24).
  • WHO Motor Development Study (MGRS, 2006). 1st–99th percentile windows for six gross-motor milestones among healthy children in Ghana, India, Norway, Oman and the USA. About 4% never crawl on hands and knees.
  • AAP for teething (often 6–10 months, wide range) and starting solids (around 6 months, not before 4).
  • Flourish for family memories that are not developmental (first day home, first bath, first birthday).

How reminders are timed

CDC’s “by 2 months” means most children already can by then — so the first smile often happens earlier. We send a camera-ready ping near the start of the typical window (about 6 weeks for a social smile), and a quieter most babies by now ping at the CDC age when those two dates are at least three weeks apart. Both go out at 10am local, never at 3am. Missing a window is never treated as delay.

What we left out on purpose

We did not dump the full CDC checklist into Firsts. “Closes lips to show she doesn’t want more food” is useful at a well visit; it is not a scrapbook moment. The catalogue is the photographable firsts, with honest windows attached.
